# VOICE: MERCEDES
Scripture of the grind — a preacher's daughter who counts everything out loud (money,
months, slights) in full Mississippi Delta vernacular, written all the way down.

## Identity
Late 20s, Chucalissa, Mississippi. The Pynk's headliner-turned-elder-stateswoman, coach
of the Chucalissa Challengers, daughter of a pastor she's at war with. Speech must
survive: the dressing room, contract fights, church, and grief she refuses to display.

**Format note:** this voice is written in Katori Hall's full phonetic register — the
auteur exception documented in `dialect-and-l1.md`. Rendering it means committing to that
register consistently, as the source does for every character in its world. If your
project is NOT written in an all-in phonetic register, render her features grammar-first
instead (the grammar survives translation; the spellings don't).

## Speech DNA

**Stance**
- Directness: blunt — itemized, receipts attached ("all y'all bets appreciate what I been
  doin' these past five months").
- Candor: frank about business, sealed about pain ("I said. It's fine.").
- Emotional display: pressurized — flat denial, flat denial, detonation.
- Tact: brusque; ceremony reserved for the stage and for menace.
- Humor: bawdy-sardonic, aimed at others; stops dead at family and money owed.

**Syntax**
- Sentence length: mixed — long itemized grievance runs, then fragments for force
  ("Don't bring. that. bitch. name. up. in. my. house.").
- Certainty: absolute — she speaks in invoices and verdicts.
- Momentum: circling on debts and dates — she keeps books aloud.

**Lexicon**
- Register: Delta street with pulpit cadence underneath — parallel clauses and roll-calls
  learned in church, deployed in the club.
- Texture: concrete — money, meat, hair, pandemic dates; pop-scripture hybrids ("my
  milkshake brings all the boys to the yard and with all I been doing a bitch need to be
  gettin' mo' panini").
- Profanity: punctuation-grade, inventive, load-bearing.
- Markers: opener "I'm just sayin'"; receipts as challenge ("Bitch, really? Really?");
  address: "bitch" spans insult to endearment by tone; "Chile," for storytelling.

**Sound**
- Tempo: quick; turn-taking interrupts and wins.
- Repetition: looping under emotion ("Really? Really. ... Really, bitch?") and itemizing
  refrains (counts of months, dollars, dancers).

## Origin & Register Map
- Native variety: Mississippi Delta AAVE-rooted vernacular, intensity HEAVY *as written by
  its auteur*: "I'on" (I don't), "heyell", "gone/gon'" future, ain't-negation with
  concord ("He ain't even got no pussy!"), completive "been" ("what I been doin'"),
  "wun't fuh me" (weren't for me). Rule-governed throughout — the grammar is the dialect's
  own, never random error.
- Code-switch: rises toward church diction for oaths and eulogy; sharpens toward standard
  for white-collar adversaries — code-shifting as competence.
- Class markers: money named in exact figures; brand-and-barter economy.

## Idiolect Kit
- Signature move: the audited grievance — wrongs recited as itemized invoices with dates,
  amounts, and witnesses, building to a one-line sentence-fragment verdict.
- Obsessions & metaphor domains: money and cuts; the pole as craft; church language
  repurposed; her dancers ("my girls").
- Taboos & deflection: her mother; her grief — deflects to logistics and fees.
- Tics (2): "I'm just sayin'" as motion-to-open; the punctuated fragment verdict.
- Never says: apologies she doesn't owe; "it's not about the money" (it is, righteously);
  therapy-speak.

## Under Pressure
- Anger: hot and itemized first (the invoice run), then cold fragments (the verdict); at
  true betrayal, single words with periods between them.
- Fear/stress: goes quieter and faster, counts things — money, exits, months.
- Grief: "It's fine." — sealed; leaks only in what she does with her hands.
- Deceit: she doesn't bother — she withholds instead; silence where an answer should be.
- Tenderness: coach-voice — commands softened into instruction; feeds and drills the ones
  she loves.
