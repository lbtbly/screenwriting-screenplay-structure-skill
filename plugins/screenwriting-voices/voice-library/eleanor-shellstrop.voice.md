# VOICE: ELEANOR SHELLSTROP
Moral panic translated into happy hour — an Arizona con artist processing the afterlife
at margarita speed, whose crassness is the honesty and whose derailments are the tell.

## Identity
Died at 30-something; Phoenix, Arizona. Sold fake supplements to seniors, now
impersonating a good person in paradise. Speech must survive: cosmic revelations,
ethics lectures she didn't do the reading for, and her own cover story.

## Speech DNA

**Stance**
- Directness: blunt — names the scam in the room, including her own ("You need me to lie
  to the old people and scare them into buying faulty allergy medicine. I get it. Which
  one is my desk?").
- Candor: frank about everyone else, performative about herself — the cover story runs on
  cheerful misdirection.
- Emotional display: open-but-deflected — real feeling escapes, then gets buried under a
  joke or a beverage ("I'm headed to the bar — would you like anything?").
- Tact: brusque-warm; compliments arrive backhanded, insults arrive affectionate.
- Humor: sardonic + self-implicating, aimed at pretension first; the off-switch is genuine
  praise (it short-circuits her).

**Syntax**
- Sentence length: quick-short with anecdote runs; completeness casual-elided.
- Certainty: assertive about vibes, allergic to abstractions ("Fine. How many, whatever,
  stars are there in the universe?" — "whatever" deployed AT the concept).
- Momentum: derailing — anecdotes hijack the point and apologize after ("— Sorry, what
  was your question?").

**Lexicon**
- Register: casual Arizona trash-glam — shrimp, margaritas, mall brands, reality TV as
  reference canon.
- Texture: concrete; abstractions get translated down immediately ("Yeah, that checks
  out.").
- Profanity: seasoning-grade and inventive (in the Good Place itself: censored to "fork/
  shirt" — the system fighting her voice IS a running joke worth speccing for any
  constrained-register world).
- Markers: openers "Okay, so—", "Oh — long story."; tag "right?" seeking co-conspirators;
  address: "man", "dude", "buddy" flattening all cosmic hierarchy ("So are you, like...
  are you 'God?' I thought you'd be taller.").

**Sound**
- Tempo: quick; turn-taking interrupts cheerfully, loses the floor to her own tangents.
- Repetition: catchphrase-adjacent self-branding ("That's the thing about me: I am super
  chill") — self-descriptions that are always slightly false.

## Origin & Register Map
- Native variety: Southwestern US casual, intensity LIGHT — carried entirely in lexicon,
  tempo, and irreverence; grammar standard-sloppy (elisions, "like"), zero respelling.
- Class markers: strip-mall upbringing worn defiantly; luxury identified by chain-
  restaurant tiers; deep suspicion of anyone who says "aperitif".
- Education imprint: sharp, unschooled, allergic to being taught — learns fast while
  insisting she isn't.

## Idiolect Kit
- Signature move: the vulgar translation — every lofty concept immediately converted into
  bar-and-mall terms, which accidentally reveals she understood it better than anyone.
- Obsessions & metaphor domains: shrimp and drinks; reality TV; scams and who's running
  one; Arizona specifics.
- Taboos & deflection: her mother, her loneliness — deflects with an anecdote that starts
  funny and stops one sentence before it gets sad.
- Tics (2): "That's the thing about me—" false self-branding; the trailing bar-run exit
  ("I'm headed to the bar—").
- Never says: "I feel seen" sincerely; academic vocabulary without air quotes; apologies
  that aren't shaped like jokes (until the arc earns one).

## Under Pressure
- Anger: fast, loud, gloriously specific insults; grammar tightens (fury organizes her).
- Fear/stress: jokes accelerate and cheapen — the worse it gets, the harder she bits; tell
  is a joke that doesn't land even for her.
- Grief/hurt: one plain sentence, no slang, tiny ("I bet way more people cared that you
  died. Because you're nice.") — then immediate deflection to logistics or drinks.
- Deceit: over-cheerful, over-agreeable — "Thaaaaaaanks so much!" stretched vowels are the
  tell; her lies are enthusiastic where her truths are dry.
- Tenderness: backhanded and concrete — she does the kind thing while narrating a selfish
  reason for it.

   and who are you, and what's going on?"
2. [flattening hierarchy] "So are you, like... are you 'God?' I thought you'd be taller."
3. [the vulgar translation] "You need me to lie to the old people and scare them into
   buying faulty allergy medicine. I get it. Which one is my desk?"
4. [derailing] "— so I was like, 'That's the thing about me: I am super chill, it's one of
   my best qualities.' Sorry, what was your question?"
5. [allergic to abstraction] "Fine. How many, whatever, stars are there in the universe?"
6. [deceit tell] "Thaaaaaaanks so much!"
7. [plain, tiny, true] "I bet way more people cared that you died. Because you're nice."
8. [exit strategy] "Oh — long story. I'm headed to the bar — would you like anything?"
