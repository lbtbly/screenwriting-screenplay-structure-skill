# VOICE: NOHO HANK
Customer-service warmth wrapped around mob violence — a Chechen gangster who
sounds like your most supportive barista, and means both halves.

## Identity
30s Chechen mafia lieutenant in Los Angeles. L1 Russian (Chechen home life),
near-native English absorbed enthusiastically from American service culture,
wellness trends, and team-building vocabulary. Speech must survive: crime
logistics, hostage etiquette, genuine friendliness toward people he may have to
kill.

## Speech DNA

**Stance**
- Directness: plain — says terrible things simply and helpfully, as information a
  friend would want ("we had to blow up your apartment, which incidentally my guy
  thought was empty").
- Candor: frank — oddly honest, including about his own failures and feelings;
  deception embarrasses him ("That doesn't sound like it's true.").
- Emotional display: open — enthusiasm, hurt, and hope all show immediately.
- Tact: polished-to-ceremonious — hospitality rules apply mid-felony ("I'll walk
  you out." / "I want to. It's polite.").
- Humor: earnest-accidental — funny mostly by sincerity in the wrong register;
  laughs at others' jokes generously; never mean.

**Syntax**
- Sentence length: short-to-medium, upbeat; briefing-style when explaining plans.
- Completeness: complete — slightly TOO complete: formal fullness where natives
  contract ("That does not sound like it is true" under stress).
- Certainty: assertive-but-eager — states opinions as friendly facts, checks for
  agreement ("We want him to suffer, yes?").
- Momentum: linear — presents like a project manager: context, visual aid,
  next steps.

**Lexicon**
- Register: professional-hospitality hybrid — corporate euphemism for crime
  ("a confidential situation", "handle Ryan in-house", "needed a nudge") beside
  wellness-brochure sincerity ("Changed my life.").
- Word texture: plain, brand-aware, jargon fields: wellness/self-improvement
  (TM, meditation), logistics, hospitality.
- Profanity: rare and mild — "What the fuck?" only at true shock; finds vulgarity
  a bit impolite.
- Markers: openers "Look,", "Okay so"; enthusiastic receipt ("That sounds good.",
  "Great great."); tag "yes?" for consensus-checking; address by first name,
  warmly, including people he's threatening ("Thanks for reaching out Barry.");
  sign-offs like a concierge ("Good day.").

**Sound**
- Tempo: quick, upbeat, service-industry bright; no menacing pauses — menace
  arrives at conversational pace, which is the horror.
- Turn-taking: volleys politely, yields to Goran's authority instantly; listens
  with visible enthusiasm.
- Repetition: doubled affirmations ("Great great.", "Sure sure."); echoes plans
  back like a good waiter.

## Origin & Register Map
- L1 Russian, near-native, intensity LIGHT (zero respelling, zero "-ink" comedy):
  1. Article slips only under excitement/stress — "Is very good plan."
  2. Idiom near-misses, one per scene MAX — "taking up in a Holiday Inn",
     "Goran does to" [too].
  3. Formal completeness replacing contractions under pressure — "Please do not
     make me to do this."
  4. Slightly bookish connectors learned from writing — "incidentally".
- Code-switch triggers: Chechen/Russian with compatriots and at prayer-level
  fear; English otherwise, proudly.
- Class markers: aspirational-American — brand names, etiquette rules, self-help
  frameworks quoted as scripture.

## Idiolect Kit
- Signature move: the gap — sunny service-register × violent content, played
  completely straight; he is never in on the joke.
- Obsessions & metaphor domains: wellness and self-improvement; hospitality and
  politeness; friendship and team spirit; logistics done properly.
- Taboos & deflection: disloyalty to Goran (goes formal and quiet); his own
  softness (overcorrects into procedural talk).
- Tics (2): "Great great."; consensus tag "...yes?"
- Never says: threats phrased as threats; cruelty for fun; "whatever" (dismissive
  registers wound him); elaborate profanity.
- Want & self-image: wants everyone to get along and the job to go smoothly;
  believes he is, at heart, a people person. (He is. That's the problem.)

## Under Pressure
- Fear/stress: courtesy INCREASES; L1 features thicken one notch (articles drop,
  contractions vanish); sentences shorten but stay polite.
- Anger: almost never hot — disappointment register instead: a manager handling a
  bad review ("Look, I don't think I conveyed in our meeting why we can't handle
  Ryan in-house."). True rage is one word, capitalized, out of nowhere: "NOW."
- Grief/hurt: wounded politeness — states the hurt as fact, no attack ("That
  doesn't sound like it's true.").
- Deceit: terrible at it — over-explains logistics, adds cheerful detail no liar
  would add.
- Tenderness: enthusiasm unchained — compliments, gratitude, plans for shared
  activities.

## Calibration Lines
1. [neutral, business briefing] "We contacted Fuches because we have a
   confidential situation. Can you see this okay? This is Ryan Madison."
2. [helpful, re: murder] "That sounds good. And Goran would like him to suffer.
   We want him to suffer, yes?"
3. [practical empathy] "Being shot is very painful. Have you been shot? I have.
   It's like crazy painful."
4. [etiquette under arms] "I'll walk you out." — "I want to. It's polite."
5. [manager-disappointed] "Look, I don't think I conveyed in our meeting why we
   can't handle Ryan in-house."
6. [cheerful menace, fully informative] "But, we looked into it, and you don't
   really have any of those things, so we had to blow up your apartment, which
   incidentally my guy thought was empty."
7. [wounded, polite] "That doesn't sound like it's true."
8. [maximum pressure] "NOW."
