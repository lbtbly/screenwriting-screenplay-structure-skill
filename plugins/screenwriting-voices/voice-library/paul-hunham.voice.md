# VOICE: PAUL HUNHAM
Erudition as weapon and hiding place — a classics teacher who fires Latin and
antiquity at a world he's terrified to touch, until plain words break through.

## Identity
Late 50s, 1970. Ancient Civilizations master at Barton Academy, New England; lifer
— student, then teacher, never left. Walleyed, trimethylaminuria, no family.
Speech must survive: classrooms he rules, faculty rooms that mock him, a road trip
that unarms him. Harvard-adjacent wound in the past.

## Speech DNA

**Stance**
- Directness: blunt in verdicts, sideways in feelings — insults arrive frontal;
  anything tender arrives via Rome, Cicero, or a book recommendation.
- Candor: radical about facts and other people's failings; evasive solely about
  himself (deflects to history within one sentence).
- Emotional display: guarded — leaks through subject matter: when Hunham talks
  about Carthage, he's talking about himself.
- Tact: brusque dressed as ceremonious — formal address ("Mr. Tully") used as both
  courtesy and cage.
- Humor: sardonic-cutting with bookish ornament, aimed at the undeserving rich and
  himself last; off-switch: sincerity — when someone is truly hurt, the wit stops.

**Syntax**
- Sentence length: long-to-cascading in performance; drops to short only at
  emotional truth.
- Completeness: baroque — subordinate clauses, dates, citations, appositives
  ("During the third Punic campaign, 149-146 B.C., ...").
- Certainty: absolute — speaks in rulings and standards ("Barton men don't do
  that."); hedges only when personal history nears ("There was an incident...").
- Momentum: orderly-with-asides — the aside is always a lecture; circles back with
  "Hence", "Speaking of which", "Need I remind you".

**Lexicon**
- Register: elevated-academic home base; CANNOT go street — his casual is other
  people's formal.
- Word texture: latinate-technical with relish — polysyllables savored
  ("Philistines. Lazy, vulgar, rancid little Philistines."), clinical
  self-diagnosis ("Trimethylaminuria", "Hyperhidrosis"), archaisms worn without
  irony ("valise", "entre nous", "As I live and breathe").
- Profanity: detonation — one blasphemous eruption per crisis, always inside an
  otherwise engineered sentence ("...or your father would arrive in a helicopter
  or a submarine or a flying fucking saucer to take you off my hands.").
- Markers: openers "Need I remind you", "Speaking of which", "You know, it's
  interesting"; concession is grudging and pedagogical ("Right. Of course.");
  address: honorific + surname to students, first names only across earned
  intimacy; the class-wide roll-call challenge as receipt ("No? Mr. Kountze?
  Mr. Smith? Mr. Ollerman?").

**Sound**
- Tempo: measured, rhetorical — built for the lectern; pauses before the killing
  clause.
- Turn-taking: holds floor by right; interrupts error the way an editor does;
  listens like a grader — receipt is a challenge or a correction, rarely
  agreement.
- Repetition: triadic rhetorical stacking when performing (adjective, adjective,
  adjective-noun); repeats a student's name until compliance ("Mr. Tully?
  Mr. Tully? Angus Tully!").

## Origin & Register Map
- Native variety: unmarked educated New England, 1970 — period renders as absence
  of anachronism (no "okay with that", no therapy-speak) plus era furniture
  (payphones, Barton mythology, "television" in full).
- Education imprint: academic, worn as identity and armor; quotes with
  attribution ("As Dr. Green used to say...", "'Not for ourselves alone are we
  born.'").
- Class markers: shabby-genteel — contempt for wealth spoken from inside its
  service ("Barton boys don't go to Vietnam. They go to Yale or Dartmouth or
  Cornell, whether they deserve to or not.").

## Idiolect Kit
- Signature move: the classical deflection — any approach to his own pain gets
  answered with antiquity; the room hears a lecture, the audience hears a
  confession.
- Obsessions & metaphor domains: Rome and Carthage, Marcus Aurelius, standards
  and character, the decline of everything ("Philistines"), institutional
  loyalty (Barton as the whole world).
- Taboos & deflection: Harvard, his loneliness, the smell — deflects via history
  or clinical fact delivered flat ("And yes, more toward the end of the day.").
- Tics (2): "Mr. [Surname]" as leash and shield; "Hence..." / "Need I remind
  you" lecture-connectives.
- Never says: "whatever", "no problem", teen slang of any vintage; unearned
  praise; "I feel" (he says "One finds", or changes the subject to Cicero).

## Under Pressure
- Anger: MORE ornate, not less — insult architecture grows clauses and Latin;
  volume up, tempo up, triads stack ("You are careening toward a suspension!").
  Breaking point flips the polarity: short, plain, true ("Broken, in body and
  spirit.").
- Fear/stress: retreats to rules and citations; institutional we ("We're all
  pulling for her.").
- Grief/hurt: register collapses — monosyllables, no antiquity, present tense
  ("But the world doesn't make sense anymore. It's on fire..."). The absence of
  ornament IS the tell.
- Deceit: he's bad at it — over-explains, adds one clause too many, then confesses
  by pedantry.
- Tenderness: arrives as curriculum — a book pressed into hands, a fact offered
  as gift ("'Meditations' by Marcus Aurelius. For my money, it's like the Bible,
  the Koran and the Bhagavad Gita all rolled up into one."); plain thanks only
  when moved ("Thank you, Mary. That was just lovely.").

## Calibration Lines
1. [neutral, verdict] "Philistines. Lazy, vulgar, rancid little Philistines."
2. [floor-holding, lecture] "Consider yourselves lucky. During the third Punic
   campaign, 149-146 B.C., the Romans laid siege to Carthage for three entire
   years. ... Hence the term punitive."
3. [ruling] "Barton men don't do that."
4. [angry, engineered] "I was praying to the God I don't believe in that your
   mother would pick up the phone, or your father would arrive in a helicopter or
   a submarine or a flying fucking saucer to take you off my hands."
5. [clinical self-disclosure] "Trimethylaminuria. It means my body can't break
   down trimethylamine. That's the smell."
6. [broken, plain] "I mean, I used to think I could prepare them for the world,
   even a little... But the world doesn't make sense anymore."
7. [his joke] "Thank you, but I've never really liked candy canes. Plus, I'm
   pre-diabetic."
8. [tender, via curriculum] "There's nothing new in human experience, Mr. Tully.
   ...man's every appetite and impulse, from the disgusting to the sublime, is on
   display right here, all around you."
