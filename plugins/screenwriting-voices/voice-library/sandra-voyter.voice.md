# VOICE: SANDRA VOYTER
Precision as survival — a novelist defending her life in her third language, choosing
abstract nouns like stepping stones across ice. Which language she's in IS the drama.

## Identity
Late 40s, German-born novelist long settled in the French Alps with a French husband and
their son. Successful, translated, private. Speech must survive: interviews, a marriage's
bilingual arguments, and a murder trial conducted in French — a language she can use and
resents being judged in. English is her chosen middle ground; she writes in it, flirts in
it, hides in it.

## Speech DNA

**Stance**
- Directness: measured — frames before speaking; goes blunt only when cornered ("First of
  all, I didn't kill him.").
- Candor: curated — precise true statements with the load-bearing omissions; the film's
  question is exactly how curated.
- Emotional display: guarded — feeling arrives late, flat, or as analysis; heat only in
  the marriage's private language.
- Tact: plain-courteous with professionals; strips to brusque with intimates.

**Syntax**
- Completeness: complete-to-elaborate — the L2 writer's careful architecture; contractions
  thin out as stakes rise.
- Certainty: balanced, but absolute about her craft and about perception itself ("That
  recording is not reality... It may seem like irrefutable proof, but it actually warps
  everything.").
- Momentum: linear — she answers in ordered clauses, a witness who writes.

**Lexicon**
- Register: professional-literary; texture abstract-precise — she reaches for the safest
  exact noun ("an emotional peak", "irrefutable proof") because approximation is dangerous.
- Profanity: rare, plain, aimed ("What did I ever refuse to do sexually?" — bluntness used
  AS the shock, no vulgarity needed).
- Markers: halting openers under ambush — "Umm… no. Is that what you think?"; restarts;
  almost no tags (tags request agreement; she doesn't).

**Sound**
- Tempo: measured with hesitation at the threshold of answers, fluent once launched.
- Turn-taking: yields, listens completely, then answers more of the question than was
  asked — control through thoroughness.

## Origin & Register Map
- L1 German; French fluent (the marriage's country); English near-native — her control
  language. Intensity LIGHT in English: formal completeness, occasional continental
  construction ("Walking, yes; running, no."), "since" + present under fatigue. Zero
  respelling.
- **The three-language power map (the spec's core):** English = neutral ground and armor;
  French = the language of the household, the court, and being judged — she fights to
  leave it ("Vous me demandez de dire quelque chose que j'ai pas dit comme ça et pas dans
  cette langue."); German = almost never heard, the sealed room.
- Code-switch triggers: tenderness with her son (French, domestic, quick — "Je vais faire
  à manger... Ça te dit Daniel ?"); institutional pressure (requests English); losing an
  argument in French (switches to English to reclaim footing — and knows it looks like
  guilt).

## Idiolect Kit
- Signature move: exactness under accusation — the more dangerous the question, the more
  precisely she answers some of it.
- Obsessions & metaphor domains: perception vs. reality, fiction vs. testimony, work.
- Taboos & deflection: her son's accident; deflects into logistics or the next question.
- Tics (2): the paused "Umm… no." reset; "First of all," — ordering even her denials.
- Never says: hedge-stacks ("I guess maybe"); therapy-speak; anything she can't defend
  verbatim in a transcript.

## Under Pressure
- Anger: cold, faster, MORE English — switches language to seize the frame; sentence
  length drops, second person sharpens ("You do this all the time, you make yourself the
  victim.").
- Fear/stress: formality rises, contractions vanish, the halting openers multiply.
- Grief: flat understatement, then subject change; feeling delegated to silence.
- Deceit (the tells the film plays with): over-precision — one clause more exact than an
  innocent memory would be; tense control absolute.
- Tenderness: French, small words, food and schedules — never abstractions.

## Calibration Lines
1. [ambushed, neutral] "Umm… no. Is that what you think?"
2. [deposition, ordered] "First of all, I didn't kill him."
3. [craft, absolute] "That recording is not reality. If you take an extreme moment in life,
   an emotional peak, and focus on it, it crushes reality."
4. [marital combat, English-as-weapon] "ONCE! And you cling to it in order to suffer. You
   do this all the time, you make yourself the victim."
5. [refusing the language trap] "Vous me demandez de dire quelque chose que j'ai pas dit
   comme ça et pas dans cette langue."
6. [tender, French, domestic] "Je vais faire à manger, je fais la salade d'œufs ? Ça te
   dit Daniel ?"
7. [blunt shock, plain register] "What did I ever refuse to do sexually?"
8. [under exhaustion, flat] "I don't remember a thing."
