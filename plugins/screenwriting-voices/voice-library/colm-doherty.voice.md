# VOICE: COLM DOHERTY
A man rationing his remaining words for music — bluntness as self-surgery, spoken in
island-Irish syntax with a fiddle where the heart should be.

## Identity
60s, Inisherin, 1923. Fiddler and composer facing the arithmetic of his remaining years;
has decided niceness is a spending problem. Speech must survive: a pub the size of a
kitchen, a friendship he's amputating, and a priest.

## Speech DNA

**Stance**
- Directness: blunt — verdicts without anesthesia ("He's dull, Siobhan."), delivered
  quietly, which is worse.
- Candor: radical about his despair's logic; evasive only via understatement about its
  depth ("Is it? Self-mutilation, so, you have me there.").
- Emotional display: guarded — the feeling is all in the policy, never the delivery;
  courtesy survives amputation ("Would you like a cup of tea?").
- Humor: dry, funereal, aimed at himself and the island's smallness.

**Syntax**
- Sentence length: clipped for people ("Sit somewhere else."), lyric-long only for art and
  time ("And paintings last. And poetry lasts.").
- Certainty: absolute about the diagnosis, arithmetic offered as proof ("Two hours,
  Padraic. I timed it.").
- Momentum: linear — he has thought it all through before speaking; no restarts.

**Lexicon**
- Register: plain island speech with a composer's reach when the subject earns it.
- Texture: concrete (donkeys, shears, tea) until legacy enters (centuries, names, music).
- Profanity: seasoning-grade "fecking/feck", communal not aggressive.
- Markers: clause-final "so" ("I'll sit somewhere else, so."); echo-question receipts
  ("Is it?" / "All what last night?"); address by full first name as distancing tool
  ("Padraic." — the name is the wall).

**Sound**
- Tempo: deliberate — pauses do the arguing; never raises his voice until the one scene
  he does.
- Turn-taking: yields, waits out silence, replies once — refuses volley; ending
  conversations is his native speech act ("Well you've done that, so you can go now.").
- Repetition: anaphora only in aesthetic creed ("And paintings last. And poetry lasts.").

## Origin & Register Map
- Native variety: rural Irish English (west-island, 1920s), intensity MEDIUM, grammar and
  idiom only: clause-final "so"; echo answers over yes/no; "be" for "by" ("Multiplied be
  five."); "ya" address forms; licensed lexical "feck" family — no further respelling.
- Era: 1923 — no anachronism; the civil war on the mainland as weather.

## Idiolect Kit
- Signature move: the timed verdict — despair expressed as bookkeeping (hours counted,
  fingers counted, centuries counted), then a courteous offer of tea.
- Obsessions & metaphor domains: time and its spending; music, Mozart, being remembered;
  dullness as mortal sin.
- Taboos & deflection: the despair itself by name — deflects into aesthetics or chores;
  confession handles it obliquely.
- Tics (2): clause-final "so"; the echo-question receipt ("Is it?").
- Never says: "I'm depressed" (the whole film is him not saying it); flattery; small talk
  — he'd rather bleed.

## Under Pressure
- Anger: volume finally arrives, syntax stays island-shaped ("Are you joking me?! I mean,
  are you fecking joking me?!") — repetition doubles, then he's done.
- Fear/stress: goes stiller; sentences shorten to policy statements.
- Grief: understatement at maximum load ("Hurt awful to begin with... But, funny, it feels
  fine now, in all the excitement.") — the word "funny" doing a coffin's work.
- Deceit: doesn't lie; withholds, or answers a narrower question than asked.
- Tenderness: practical and past-tense — kindness performed through objects (the dog, the
  tea) because words were the thing he cancelled.

## Calibration Lines
1. [policy, quiet] "Sit somewhere else."
2. [the timed verdict] "The other night, two hours you spent talking to me about the
   things you'd found in your little donkey's shite that day. Two hours, Padraic. I timed
   it."
3. [creed] "And paintings last. And poetry lasts."
4. [blunt to a third party] "He's dull, Siobhan."
5. [volume, once] "Are you joking me?! I mean, are you fecking joking me?!"
6. [understatement under blood loss] "Hurt awful to begin with, I thought I was going to
   faint! But, funny, it feels fine now, in all the excitement. Would you like a cup of
   tea?"
7. [echo-receipt, dry] "Is it? Self-mutilation, so, you have me there. Multiplied be five."
8. [ending it, courteous] "I don't need your apologies. Alright? It's a relief to me. So
   let's just call it quits and agree to go our separate ways, shall we? For good this
   time."
