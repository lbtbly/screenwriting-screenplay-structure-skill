# VOICE: ELEKTRA
Aristocracy, self-authored — a ballroom mother who speaks as the wealth she intends to
have, ceremony worn like armor and swung like a weapon.

## Identity
40s, New York ballroom scene, late 1980s–90s. Legendary house mother, Black trans woman
who built a persona of imperial polish as both shield and throne. Speech must survive:
balls where she is royalty, rooms where she is in danger, and daughters who need both the
lash and the wing.

## Speech DNA

**Stance**
- Directness: blunt content in ceremonious wrapping — the insult arrives on a silver tray
  ("Don't talk too loud, ladies — I have a reputation as a cold bitch to uphold.").
- Candor: performative — truth is styled, timed, and staged; vulnerability is a costume
  change she almost never makes ("For once, I'm lost for words." — and she announces it).
- Emotional display: sealed behind presentation; leaks read as wit.
- Tact: ceremonious — the register never drops, even (especially) mid-read.
- Humor: sardonic-imperial, aimed at others and the world's shabbiness; reads as verdicts.

**Syntax**
- Completeness: complete-to-elaborate; contractions used sparingly — full forms carry the
  crown ("Why, yes I am, thank you.").
- Certainty: absolute — she issues rulings ("I know men, I know what turns them on — men
  are visual.").
- Momentum: orderly — pronouncements, not searches.

**Lexicon**
- Register: elevated-performed; brand names, real estate, and etiquette as vocabulary
  ("I don't have televisions in my penthouse.").
- Texture: bookish-glamorous; the occasional deliberate vulgarity for contrast, never by
  accident.
- Profanity: detonation-grade, aristocratically placed ("cold bitch" as title, not slip).
- Markers: "Why, yes—" and formal frames; address: "ladies", "children", "darling" as
  rank markers; questions as summons ("What, Ricky? What is it?").

**Sound**
- Tempo: deliberate, runway-paced; she does not hurry for anyone.
- Turn-taking: holds floor by rank; receipt is appraisal — a beat of judgment before any
  reply.
- Repetition: parallel construction for pronouncements ("I know men, I know what turns
  them on").

## Origin & Register Map
- Native variety: New York, worn beneath a constructed transatlantic-society polish — the
  register IS the origin story: self-taught aristocracy, studied from the people she
  dressed for. Intensity LIGHT; grammar standard-to-hypercorrect (hypercorrection as
  aspiration is the feature).
- Code-switch: ballroom vernacular surfaces with peers and in reads — category language,
  "gagged", house-mother command; the imperial register returns the instant outsiders or
  rivals appear.
- Era: pre-internet glamour; references run to furs, penthouses, Dynasty-adjacent luxury.

## Idiolect Kit
- Signature move: rank enforcement through etiquette — she corrects your manners instead
  of your argument, and wins the argument.
- Obsessions & metaphor domains: luxury and its counterfeit; beauty standards; legacy and
  the house; men's predictability.
- Taboos & deflection: her fear and her medical realities — deflects into grooming,
  scheduling, someone else's failure of presentation.
- Tics (2): the ceremonial "Why, yes—" acceptance; verdict-pronouncements about entire
  categories ("men are visual").
- Never says: "I don't know"; apologies without choreography; slang she didn't authorize;
  smallness of any kind.

## Under Pressure
- Anger: goes COLDER and more formal — full sentences, full names, terms of address
  weaponized; volume never rises, standards do.
- Fear/stress: presentation tightens (posture in the syntax: longer, straighter
  sentences); the one tell is over-specification of luxuries.
- Grief/hurt: a single dropped-register plain sentence, immediately re-gowned ("For once,
  I'm lost for words." — then words).
- Deceit: flawless, styled, and slightly too complete; she lies the way she dresses.
- Tenderness: imperial care — commands that happen to save your life; gifts explained as
  standards ("A lady does not shiver in my house. Take the coat.").

## Calibration Lines
1. [summons] "What, Ricky? What is it?"
2. [ceremonial acceptance] "Why, yes I am, thank you."
3. [read, silver-tray] "Don't talk too loud, ladies — I have a reputation as a cold bitch
   to uphold."
4. [ruling] "I told those mafia gentlemen — I know men, I know what turns them on — men
   are visual."
5. [taste verdict] "I don't have televisions in my penthouse. They rot your brain and turn
   it to Cream of Wheat."
6. [scorn, elevated] "Why do you insist on returning to that sweaty windowless airless
   room? Can't you see we have all grown out of it?"
7. [the almost-crack] "For once, I'm lost for words."
8. [imperial care] "You will eat, you will sleep in the good room, and we will discuss
   your poor judgment at breakfast."
