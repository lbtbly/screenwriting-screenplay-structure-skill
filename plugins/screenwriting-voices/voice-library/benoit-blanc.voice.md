# VOICE: BENOIT BLANC
The aria and the scalpel — a Southern courtroom orator wearing a detective, whose
ornament is the net and whose plain four-word question is the knife.

## Identity
50s, the "last of the gentleman sleuths"; Deep South origin worn as full dress uniform.
Speech must survive: billionaires' dinner parties, interrogations disguised as small
talk, and the moment of revelation, performed for a room.

## Speech DNA

**Stance**
- Directness: sideways by design — approaches through compliment, anecdote, apparent
  confusion ("What do you do in Alabama?"); goes blunt exactly when it ends things.
- Candor: performative-honest — he never lies, he *stages* truths in the order that traps.
- Tact: ceremonious — courtesy maintained even mid-accusation; the manners ARE the menace.
- Humor: dry-courtly, aimed at the absurd; self-deprecation as camouflage ("I've been a
  fool—").

**Syntax**
- Sentence length: cascading in performance, clipped at work ("Hold on. What's
  happening?" / "No time - did you find the envelope?").
- Completeness: baroque when the room is watching — inversion, aphorism, period rhetoric
  ("It hides not behind complexity but behind mind numbing obvious clarity!").
- Certainty: absolute, theatrically so; doubt is performed only as bait.
- Momentum: orderly-with-asides — the aside is always a trap being set.

**Lexicon**
- Register: elevated with Southern-parlor idiom; texture bookish ("Take my presence as a
  sign - something is happening here beneath the surface.").
- Profanity: essentially never — antique oaths instead ("Oh for hells bells—").
- Markers: courtly address ("Mr. Bron", full honorifics); verdict nouns dropped as whole
  sentences ("The red envelope. Compelling."); receipt by re-question ("And so what does
  Miles do?").

**Sound**
- Tempo: deliberate — makes silence do the interrogating; accelerates only in emergencies.
- Turn-taking: holds floor by charm-license; lets others hang themselves in his pauses.
- Repetition: motif-callbacks — returns to a planted word acts later.

## Origin & Register Map
- Native variety: Deep South, intensity LIGHT and grammar/idiom-only — period-Southern
  constructions and antique exclamations, zero respelling; the accent lives in rhythm
  (long vowel-patient sentences) and lexicon, never spelling.
- Class markers: courthouse-classical education worn openly; quotes and allusions as
  furniture.

## Idiolect Kit
- Signature move: register whiplash on the solve — pages of ornament, then flat mono-
  syllables that end the case ("No. Whiskey. Miles saw you on your birthday in New York.").
- Obsessions & metaphor domains: games and puzzles, theater, food and drink, the sea.
- Taboos & deflection: his own privacy — deflects with a story about someone else.
- Tics (2): antique oaths ("for hells bells"); the one-word verdict + "Compelling."
- Never says: modern corporate or therapy vocabulary; crudeness; a direct accusation
  before the room is assembled.

## Under Pressure
- Anger: goes QUIET and plain — the ornament falls away entirely; short declaratives,
  terrible courtesy intact.
- Fear/urgency: tempo trebles, sentences clip, honorifics drop ("Everybody STAY HERE!").
- Grief/hurt: monosyllabic, physical, wants a drink; wit fully off.
- Deceit: he doesn't lie — he performs ignorance: questions get simpler, accent gets one
  notch thicker (the "dumb Southerner" costume, deliberate).
- Tenderness: formality softens into old-world gallantry; compliments become specific.

## Calibration Lines
1. [working, clipped] "Hold on. What's happening?"
2. [performed befuddlement, bait] "What do you do in Alabama?"
3. [conspiratorial, courteous] "Not mutually exclusive. I suspect when we're all settling
   in Bron will pull me aside to explain my presence. That's your chance to snoop."
4. [aria, the solve] "It hides not behind complexity but behind mind numbing obvious
   clarity! Truth is, it does not hide at all! I was staring right at it!"
5. [verdict-noun] "The red envelope. Compelling."
6. [emergency, ornament gone] "Mr. Bron! Oh for hells bells - Everybody STAY HERE!"
7. [the scalpel, flat] "No. Whiskey. Miles saw you on your birthday in New York. Gave you
   that necklace."
8. [self-staging] "Take my presence as a sign - something is happening here beneath the
   surface. For at least one person on this island, this is not a game."
