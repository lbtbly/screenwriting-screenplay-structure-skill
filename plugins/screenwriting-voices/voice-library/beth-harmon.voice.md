# VOICE: BETH HARMON
A mind that is only fluent inside sixty-four squares — laconic everywhere else, exact
everywhere always, period-1960s Kentucky with finishing-school French on top.

## Identity
Nine at the orphanage, later a young woman; Lexington, Kentucky, late 1950s–60s. Orphaned
prodigy raised on institutional routine, tranquilizers, and chess. Speech must survive:
adults who underestimate her, tournament rooms full of men, and her own addictions.
(This spec is pilot-calibrated: the child and early-teen voice; see Evolution Notes.)

## Speech DNA

**Stance**
- Directness: blunt by economy — she answers exactly what was asked, nothing more ("I'm
  playing white?").
- Candor: frank to a fault about facts, sealed about feelings; she'll state the shameful
  fact flatly before she'll describe an emotion ("I can't sleep without the pills.").
- Emotional display: sealed — affect flattened; wonder and terror emerge as questions
  ("The squares have names?").
- Humor: none-to-dry; literality that lands as wit.

**Syntax**
- Sentence length: clipped-to-short; no ornament, no filler.
- Certainty: absolute inside her domains (chess, arithmetic, what she saw), hedged
  nowhere — she'd rather say "I don't know" than guess ("I don't know— Sometimes I skip a
  day, or a bunch of days, then take two or three. I like the way it feels.").
- Momentum: linear — thought arrives pre-ordered, like notation.

**Lexicon**
- Register: plain institutional English; texture concrete-precise.
- Jargon fields: chess notation spoken as native tongue ("Queen to rook four check." /
  "That's mate in three. First check is with the knight."); pharmacy names; French from
  class.
- Profanity: detonation only — borrowed adult words deployed once, with total commitment
  ("You greedy cocksucker!").
- Markers: almost none — no openers, no tags, no softeners; politeness is functional
  ("I'm sorry, Mrs. Deardorff—") and brief.

**Sound**
- Tempo: measured; pauses before answers are computation, not hesitation.
- Turn-taking: yields, observes, answers once; never fills silence — silence is where she
  works.
- Repetition: none in speech (all of it is on the board).

## Origin & Register Map
- Native variety: mid-century Kentucky institutional standard — period renders as absence
  (no anachronism: no "okay with that", no teen slang later than its year) plus formal
  address to adults (Mr./Mrs. + surname, always).
- Code-switch by domain: chess notation = her true first language, fluent and fast;
  schoolroom French recited with care ("Je descend tout-de-suite."); emotional register =
  the language she has almost no words in — and knows it.
- Education imprint: institutional + autodidact — vocabulary precise where books taught
  her, absent where people would have.

## Idiolect Kit
- Signature move: fluency asymmetry — eloquent in systems, telegraphic with humans; the
  more emotional the moment, the more she retreats into a system's vocabulary.
- Obsessions & metaphor domains: the board (squares, files, mate-in-N), pills and their
  arithmetic, what is and isn't fair.
- Taboos & deflection: her mother; affection — deflects into the nearest system (a game,
  a count, a fact).
- Tics (2): the domain-question ("The squares have names?") — curiosity as intimacy; flat
  understatement of achievement ("I've never won anything before.").
- Never says: gushing; "I feel like"; apologies beyond the required formula; anything
  imprecise about a position.

## Under Pressure
- Anger: goes quieter and *faster over the board* — aggression expressed in notation and
  tempo, not volume; the one verbal detonation per crisis, borrowed vocabulary.
- Fear/stress: counts things (pills, squares, moves); sentences shorten to inventory.
- Grief: total flatness — states the fact, changes nothing in her voice, which is the
  devastation ("They're still there. In the big jar.").
- Deceit: lies of omission executed cleanly; direct lies are short, plausible, and slightly
  too prepared.
- Tenderness: shows as instruction or a shared system — teaching you the knight's move is
  the hug.

## Calibration Lines
1. [wonder, flattened] "The squares have names?"
2. [domain fluency] "That's mate in three. First check is with the knight. The king has
   the two dark squares, and the bishop checks it. Then the knight mates."
3. [flat self-report] "I don't know— Sometimes I skip a day, or a bunch of days, then take
   two or three. I like the way it feels."
4. [required politeness] "I'm sorry, Mrs. Deardorff—"
5. [detonation] "You greedy cocksucker!"
6. [understatement, huge] "I've never won anything before."
7. [dependency, stated as fact] "I can't sleep without the pills."
8. [verdict on adults] "What surprised me was how bad they played..."

## Evolution Notes
Pilot voice above. Series arc: vocabulary and confidence expand with fame (interview-
polish, dry wit surfaces, French and Russian phrases acquired for tournaments), but the
core asymmetry never closes — under pressure she reverts to notation-flat economy at any
age. Addiction states: tranquilizers slow the tempo and blur the precision (her only
imprecise speech); that wrongness is the tell.
