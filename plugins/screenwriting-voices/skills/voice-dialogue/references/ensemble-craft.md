# Ensemble Craft — Scenes With Several Voices

Writing a scene from N voice specs is not writing N solos. The scene's music is the interaction:
who talks, who doesn't, who interrupts, who wins. Do this planning BEFORE drafting; it takes two
minutes and saves the scene.

## The pre-scene sheet (five questions)

1. **Power map.** Who holds the gun — the leverage in this room (information, money, love
   withheld, an actual gun)? Where does it change hands? Mark the handoff beat: both voices shift
   there (see `rendering-craft.md`). A scene with no handoff needs a reason to exist.
2. **Objectives, everyone.** One want per character — including the ones who'll barely speak.
   Participation follows stakes: characters speak when the current beat touches their want, and a
   character with nothing at stake in this beat should be doing something, not filling turns.
3. **Contrast plan.** Before writing, line the specs up: register, verbosity, tempo, markers. If
   two voices sit within one notch on most axes, decide NOW what separates them audibly in this
   scene (different markers, different listening behavior, opposite modes) — or the cover test
   fails in exactly their exchanges.
4. **Mode cast.** Assign a starting mode per character (neutral / emotional / declarative /
   manipulative) and engineer at least one mismatch — calm vs. upset, wise vs. panicking.
   Two emotional characters is a shouting match; two declaratives is a fortune-cookie duel.
5. **Geography.** Where is everyone, physically? Space gives the silent something to do and gives
   you cutaways that keep them alive.

## Turn allocation

- **Stakes drive turns, not fairness.** Round-robin speaking order and symmetrical line counts
  are machine tells. A terse spec should be terse HERE: two words while others speechify.
- **Triangles beat pairs.** With 3+, route reactions through a third point: A pressures B, but the
  scene is really about C watching. Rotate the pairings mid-scene; don't let the usual duos hold.
- **Silence is a turn.** A character who chooses not to speak at the choice moment has changed the
  scene's path. Give every silent character one visible choice-to-not-speak; give them micro-
  interactions (an exchanged look does a full turn's work) and physical actions anchored in the
  geography.
- **Don't serialize.** A five-person scene that's secretly a chain of two-handers wastes the room
  — unless the room-watching-a-duel IS the design (a dinner-party interrogation), in which case
  render the watching: reactions, side-bets, someone refilling glasses too attentively.

## Interruption mechanics

- Interruption encodes hierarchy and intimacy: who may cut whom off, who always gets to finish,
  who talks over everyone and who yields — read it off the power map and the specs' turn-taking
  values, and let it CHANGE at the gun handoff (the new holder starts finishing their sentences).
- Notation (formatting itself defers to screenplay-write): em-dash for the cut-off, the
  interrupter picking up mid-beat; ellipsis for trailing off — these are different psychologies,
  not interchangeable marks. Dual dialogue is a heavy spice: script true overlap rarely, at peaks.
- Self-interruption belongs to specs with derailing momentum — it's voice, not noise.
- After a dense overlap passage, give the audience a conversational clearing: one clean exchange,
  or a beat of silence.

## Listening across the ensemble

Every line shows evidence of having heard the last one — or performs a motivated refusal (evasion,
topic-grab, answering the question they wish they'd been asked). In a group, receipts fan out:
one line can land on four faces, and the NEXT speaker is the one whose want it touched. The lines
should feel like Velcro — each reply designed for the exact line before it, in the replier's
voice. A scene where speeches merely alternate is intersecting monologues; the fix is almost
always to make someone actually answer, misunderstand, or steal a word the last speaker used.

Misfire is fuel: people talk past each other, answer wrong questions, hear insults that weren't
sent. Total mutual comprehension across an ensemble is a machine tell and a drama leak.

## Ensemble failure checklist (run after drafting)

- Cover test on the WHOLE scene, support cast included — receipts and all.
- Any two characters swap-able in their shared exchanges? Fix with markers/tempo/mode, not by
  adding tics.
- Line counts suspiciously even? Re-cut turns by stakes.
- Everyone equally witty, equally articulate, equally on-topic? One voice is ventriloquizing.
- Did anyone concede, evade, interrupt, stay silent, or misunderstand? If none of the five
  happened, the scene is a press conference.
- Does the scene end on a tidy button every time? Cut out mid-energy at least sometimes.
- Were the silent characters present in the last half page, or did they evaporate?
