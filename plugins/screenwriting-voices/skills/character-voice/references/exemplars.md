# Corpus Exemplars — A Study Map of the Script Library

How to use this file: when committing an axis value, read its exemplar and steal the *mechanism*,
never the line. When a voice feels flat, find the study-voice closest to its problem and read that
character's scenes in the source script. Quotes are from the user's Script Library banks,
attributed (CHARACTER, *Title*). Complements `axes.md` (different quotes, same taxonomy).

## Axis exemplars (one fresh mechanism per axis)

**1. Directness — blunt.** "He's dull, Siobhan." (COLM, *The Banshees of Inisherin*) — verdicts
without cushioning, even about a lifelong friend; the cruelty is in the economy.

**2. Candor — curated.** "I get your position Lawrence, I understand your reservations about our
bid and I respect your viewpoint..." (KENDALL, *Succession*) — three courtesies stacked as armor
plate in front of a threat; nothing false, everything strategic.

**3. Emotional display — pressurized.** "Shit. Now you've got me nervous." → later → "I said I'm
okay!" (MARY, *The Holdovers*) — the seal admits one leak, then over-closes; the denial's volume
is the confession.

**4. Tact — ceremonious (weaponized).** "Don't talk too loud, ladies — I have a reputation as a
cold bitch to uphold." (ELEKTRA, *Pose*) — formality so complete it can carry its own insult
inside it, tray service.

**5. Humor — deflection-by-reference.** "It's the same swimsuit James Bond wears in 'On Her
Majesty's Secret Service.' Can't get more masculine than that." (ANGUS, *The Holdovers*) — the
teenager's joke outsources confidence to a citation; the Espenson test says he'll do the same
thing when he's scared, and he does.

**6. Sentence length — clipped.** "Silence, Siobhan. Just silence." (COLM, *Banshees*) — the
request for quiet performed by its own shape.

**7. Completeness — fragments for force.** "Don't bring. that. bitch. name. up. in. my. house."
(MERCEDES, *P-Valley*) — punctuation breaking a sentence into blows; fragmentation as volume.

**8. Certainty — absolute (performed).** "It hides not behind complexity but behind mind numbing
obvious clarity! Truth is, it does not hide at all!" (BLANC, *Glass Onion*) — the detective's
certainty is theater with syntax to match: inversion, aphorism, exclamation.

**9. Momentum — derailing.** "— so I was like, 'That's the thing about me: I am super chill, it's
one of my best qualities.' Sorry, what was your question?" (ELEANOR, *The Good Place*) — the
anecdote eats the conversation and apologizes on the way out.

**10. Register — in-group lexicon.** "You don't shuck and jive enough for those O'fay tastes."
(MALCOLM, *One Night in Miami*) — register as membership: the sentence tells you who's inside the
room and who the room is about.

**11. Word texture — L2 abstraction.** "That recording is not reality. If you take an extreme
moment in life, an emotional peak, and focus on it, it crushes reality." (SANDRA, *Anatomy of a
Fall*) — the non-native intellectual reaches for the abstract noun because it is the *safest*
word; precision as second-language survival skill.

**12. Profanity — detonation.** "You greedy cocksucker!" (BETH, nine years old, *The Queen's
Gambit*) — one detonation, borrowed from an adult, priced by its rarity; the whole scene turns on
a single word the voice otherwise never uses.

**13. Markers — status in the connective tissue.** "Yeah. I got, there was an issue — and so my
grandfather said to come and talk and —" (GREG, *Succession*) — fillers, restarts, and the
trail-off; the grammar of a man with no leverage. Compare his uncle's "Uh-huh."

**14. Tempo — quick attack.** "Hey Tom. How's the UK operation? You still fucking that shit up for
us?" (ROMAN, *Succession*) — greeting, jab, exit before the reply lands; tempo as dominance play.

**15. Turn-taking — floor-holding as job.** "Hello, everyone, and welcome to your first day in the
afterlife! You were all, simply put: good people, who lived good lives. But how do we know...?"
(MICHAEL, *The Good Place*) — the host register: rhetorical questions that license him to keep the
floor.

**16. Repetition — the doubled concession.** "All right, fine. Fine." (SAM, *One Night in Miami*)
— saying it twice un-says it; repetition as the sound of a man not fine at all.

## Ensemble contrast studies (the differentiation masterclass)

**One Night in Miami — four voices, one hotel room.**
- MALCOLM: "Mind if I recount the story, Sam?" — the minister's frame control: courteous custody
  of the narrative.
- SAM: "You used to be such a fun cat! Now you're acting in private like you are on camera." —
  showman's velvet needle; the insult arrives as nostalgia.
- CASSIUS: "The Beatles. They're funny, but they ain't no Sam Cooke. They're more of a fad." —
  the champion's tempo: verdicts at jab speed, generosity and dismissal in one breath.
- JIM: "I'll bet you that cheap, purple suit he will." — flattest voice in the room and the surest;
  the pragmatist prices everything, including his friends' outfits.
Same room, same stakes, four unmistakable grammars. Cover the names: nothing swaps.

**Succession pilot — power spoken four ways.**
- LOGAN: "I'll stay in situ. As chairman and head of the firm." — Latin only when annexing
  territory.
- KENDALL: "This is — this is going to be a shit-storm." — the stammer under stakes, then the
  business cliché as handrail.
- ROMAN: "Hey hey hey motherfuckers!" — profane glee; power as playground.
- GREG: "I'm not playing anyone. I am Doderick." — apologetic literalism from the bottom of the
  ladder.

**The Holdovers — three registers of hurt.**
- PAUL: "Yes, I see you've brought your valise." — archaism as armor; even luggage gets 1930.
- ANGUS: "I'm not a pussy. I just don't want to end up at Fork Union paying for your mistake." —
  deflection first, truth in the second sentence; the teenager's order of operations.
- MARY: "You just told the boy 'anything.' So take the kid to Boston." — the plainest sentence in
  any scene she's in, and it always wins the argument.

**The Good Place — three cooperations that never blend.**
- ELEANOR: "You need me to lie to the old people and scare them into buying faulty allergy
  medicine. I get it. Which one is my desk?" — con-artist pragmatics: parse the scam, take the seat.
- CHIDI: "I'm getting a stomach ache. I'm in a perfect utopia, and I have a stomach ache." —
  ethics somatized; the hedger's body files the complaint.
- MICHAEL: "Yup. Surprised me too." — cosmic bureaucrat borrowing human smallness, one size too
  small.

## Origin showcases

**Sandra (*Anatomy of a Fall*) — language as battleground.** The script code-switches on power:
"Vous me demandez de dire quelque chose que j'ai pas dit comme ça et pas dans cette langue." —
she names the trap of testifying outside her L1. Her English is formal-precise (see axis 11), her
French domestic and quick ("Je vais faire à manger, je fais la salade d'œufs ? Ça te dit
Daniel ?"). The lesson for specs: give a bilingual character different *registers per language*,
and make the switch itself dramatic.

**Bella (*Poor Things*) — the developmental arc, staged.** "Bella not safe with you I think." →
"It is tricky as I am engaged to Mr Max McCandles." → "We are now the poor. Also many adventurers
have faced these odds. Robinson Crusoe for instance." Telegraphic → formal-literal → bookish; each
stage a consistent grammar, each shift earned by experience. This is the Evolution Notes section
of a voice.md, demonstrated.

**Banshees (*Banshees of Inisherin*) — Irish syntax carrying everything.** PADRAIC: "Well don't
ask a man to call up to ya at your fecking house, so, like he has nothing better to do with his
fecking time!" COLM: "Is it? Self-mutilation, so, you have me there." Clause-final *so*, echo
questions, the roundabout build — zero respelling beyond licensed "fecking", and the island is on
every line.

**P-Valley — the auteur exception in action.** "I'on even know why the heyell Big L gettin' a cut
offa Pussyland." (MERCEDES) — Katori Hall writes her own Mississippi Delta community with total
phonetic commitment, every character, every page. It works because it is insider, systematic, and
universal in its world. It is the licensed exception (see `dialect-and-l1.md`), not the default:
the default remains grammar-first.

**Borat — the heavy-L1 comic construct.** "Jagshemash. My name a Borat. My life not nice." —
article deletion and copula drop applied with perfect consistency; a caricature that works only
because the film IS the caricature. Study the system, not the license: at HEAVY intensity, the
grammar must still be rule-governed.

**Beth (*The Queen's Gambit*) — code-switch by domain.** "Je descend tout-de-suite." (finishing-
school French) beside "Queen to rook four check." (pure chess register) beside orphan-plain
English. Three registers, one child; the spec move is jargon-fields plus per-domain register.
