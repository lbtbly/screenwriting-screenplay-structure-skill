# The Voice Axes — Full Taxonomy

The parameter system behind every voice spec. 16 scaled axes in five groups (A–D scaled, E structured), plus the Engine fields and the Modulation layer. The Voice Designer HTML mirrors this file; a voice.md written by hand should too.

Every axis entry: what it controls → the five notches (left = one extreme) → rendering notes → a corpus exemplar. Quotes are from scripts in the user's Script Library, cited as (Character, *Title*).

**Two laws before any axis:**
1. **A voice is a combination, not a setting.** No single axis makes a voice; the fingerprint is the *stack* — and the one or two places where the stack is surprising (see Signature Move, section F).
2. **Axes set the baseline, states bend it.** Everything here describes the character at rest. Scenes are rarely at rest — see the Modulation layer (section G) and `rendering-craft.md` in the voice-dialogue skill.

## Group A — STANCE (what they do with truth and feeling)

### 1. directness — how straight they aim at the point
`blunt → plain → measured → sideways → coded`
- **blunt**: names the thing, no cushioning, takes the hit for it. "Raise your bid." (Logan, *Succession*)
- **plain**: says what they mean with normal social padding.
- **measured**: chooses the frame before speaking; you feel the sentence being aimed.
- **sideways**: approaches via anecdote, comparison, someone else's story. "You just be careful you don't get too used to that feeling." (Jolene, *The Queen's Gambit*, warning wrapped as advice)
- **coded**: parable, image, quotation — the point arrives by metaphor and the listener does the last step. "In the church, we used to sing the song, 'good news, the chariot is comin'.' Is that right or wrong?" (Malcolm, *One Night in Miami*)

Rendering: directness is about *aim*, not rudeness (that's tact) and not truthfulness (that's candor). A courteous character can be blunt ("I'm afraid he's dead"); a rude one can be evasive.

### 2. candor — relationship to truth
`radical → frank → curated → evasive → performative`
- **radical**: tells truths that cost them; can't not.
- **frank**: honest by default, will soften edges.
- **curated**: truthful in what they say, strategic in what they leave out. (Elinor, *Sense and Sensibility* — nothing she says is false; half of what she feels is unsaid)
- **evasive**: deflects, changes subject, answers a different question. "He didn't want to talk about it." (Sandra, *Anatomy of a Fall*)
- **performative**: truth is one tool among several; speech is engineered for effect. (Borat's whole register; a con artist; a politician mid-apology)

Rendering: candor drives *answer behavior* — the judge's question→answer adjacency metric lives here. An evasive character answering every question directly is a spec violation.

### 3. emotional display — how much interior reaches the surface
`sealed → guarded → pressurized → open → effusive`
- **sealed**: affect flattened to nothing; feeling shows only in what they do. "Earth, Mars, what's the difference?" (Miller, *The Expanse*)
- **guarded**: feeling admitted only obliquely or with delay.
- **pressurized**: contained until it isn't — leaks in bursts, then re-seals. (Mare, *Mare of Easttown*)
- **open**: names feelings without ceremony, weathers being seen.
- **effusive**: everything shows, amplified. "Well, I love it. It doesn't bother me at all. You must be starving, honey." (Kitty, *That '90s Show*)

### 4. tact — social surface
`abrasive → brusque → plain-courteous → polished → ceremonious`
- **abrasive**: insult is grammar. "Let him be, you invisible cunt." (Butcher, *The Boys*)
- **brusque**: no cruelty, no cushions either.
- **plain-courteous**: default please/thanks of the world they live in.
- **polished**: smoothness is learned and maintained; hard to ruffle.
- **ceremonious**: formality as armor or crown. "Why, yes I am, thank you." (Elektra, *Pose*)

Rendering: tact is the surface; it can sit atop any stance. NoHo Hank (*Barry*) is *polished-to-ceremonious* tact over mob-enforcer content — the gap IS the voice.

### 5. humor — the joke they'd make (select, not slider)
Styles: **none/literal · dry-deadpan · sardonic-cutting · self-deprecating · teasing-warm · bawdy · absurdist · punny-goofy · anecdotal**
Plus two settings: **target** (self / others / the world / the absurd) and **off-switch** (what kills the jokes — grief? fear? nothing?).
- dry-deadpan: "I blame the bras. They turn 'em mean." (Red, *That '90s Show*)
- bawdy: "Unh, unh, don't run from it. I'ma need you to come deep inside. But don't hit them walls, nanh." (Uncle Clifford, *P-Valley*)
- punny-goofy: "Cheese to meet you!" (Bella, *Poor Things*)
- sardonic-cutting: "Almost 40 ain't young: it's tragic." (Uncle Clifford again — two styles, one voice; list both, mark when each fires)

The Espenson test: know the joke and you know the character's stress response and grief response. If you can't write the character's joke, the voice isn't done.

## Group B — SYNTAX (how sentences get built)

### 6. sentence length
`clipped → short → mixed → long → cascading`
- **clipped**: ≤5 words dominant. "Not now." (Logan)
- **short**: single clauses, quick full stops.
- **mixed**: natural variance (most voices; specify only if an extreme).
- **long**: multi-clause but controlled.
- **cascading**: sentences that keep finding another clause, breath-defying. "Took the express-lane through the five stages of dying -- an hour or two on denial, a few days on anger, skipped right over bargaining to depression then after a week I'd found acceptance." (Pray Tell, *Pose*)

### 7. completeness
`fragments → elided → complete → elaborate → baroque`
- **fragments**: verbless, subject-dropped shards. "So Bomie, no see. Forgotten arm." (Miller)
- **elided**: casual contractions and drops ("You okay?").
- **complete**: full grammatical sentences.
- **elaborate**: subordinate clauses, parenthesis, appositives.
- **baroque**: nested architecture that shows off its own engineering. "During the third Punic campaign, 149-146 B.C., the Romans laid siege to Carthage for three entire years. ... Hence the term punitive." (Hunham, *The Holdovers*)

### 8. certainty — hedging vs. assertion
`hedging → qualified → balanced → assertive → absolute`
- **hedging**: maybe/sort of/I guess stack up; requests permission to have opinions. "Maybe it's a test. Maybe if you go to Michael and tell him the truth, you'll pass the test..." (Chidi, *The Good Place*)
- **qualified**: one softener per claim.
- **balanced**: hedges when unsure, asserts when sure — calibrated.
- **assertive**: declaratives, few qualifiers.
- **absolute**: speaks in laws. "I know men, I know what turns them on -- men are visual." (Elektra)

### 9. momentum — organization of thought
`linear → orderly-with-asides → circling → digressive → derailing`
- **linear**: A then B then C. "One square diagonal plus one square straight." (Beth, *The Queen's Gambit*)
- **orderly-with-asides**: structured, permits one detour, returns.
- **circling**: keeps returning to the same spot from new angles (obsession made audible).
- **digressive**: the tangent is the point; arrives late if at all.
- **derailing**: interrupts own sentences, abandons clauses mid-flight, restarts. "Fine - but just in here - yeah? ... Just - a -" (Logan). Also the public walk-back: "Okay, what happened was I used the word... Retard, yes. Calling - For educational purposes only a humorous context. No, that is why I'm apologising now..." (Selina, *Veep*)

## Group C — LEXICON (which words)

### 10. register
`street → casual → standard → professional → elevated`
- **street**: the vernacular of their block, unlaundered. "Had Henney, a blunt and a nut. I'on need nuthin' else." (Mercedes, *P-Valley*)
- **casual**: relaxed general speech.
- **standard**: neutral, placeless.
- **professional**: shaped by an occupation's idiom (legal, medical, corporate, military).
- **elevated**: literary, ornate, performed. "I don't have televisions in my penthouse. They rot your brain and turn it to Cream of Wheat." (Elektra)

Rendering: register is the *home* setting; many voices command a range and shift by listener (see code-switching in `dialect-and-l1.md`). Spec the home register and the range endpoints.

### 11. word texture — size and abstraction
`concrete-plain → plain → mixed → bookish → latinate-technical`
- **concrete-plain**: things you can drop on your foot. "Rolling Rock and a shot of Jameson." (Mare)
- **bookish**: reads; it shows. Allusions, "whom", titles of things.
- **latinate-technical**: polysyllabic precision, jargon fields. "Trimethylaminuria. It means my body can't break down trimethylamine." (Hunham)
Include **jargon fields** (free list): the professions/subcultures whose vocabulary leaks into everything — chess for Beth, ballroom for Elektra, scripture for Malcolm.

### 12. profanity
`never → detonation → seasoning → punctuation → baroque`
- **never**: absence is characterization (what do they say instead? "H-E-double-hockey-sticks"? "Heyell"?).
- **detonation**: so rare each one is an event. "...or your father would arrive in a helicopter or a submarine or a flying fucking saucer..." (Hunham — the effing lands because it's the only one in the reel)
- **seasoning**: casual, low-heat.
- **punctuation**: structural, rhythmic, unremarkable to the speaker. (Mercedes, Mare)
- **baroque**: profanity as craft — compound, inventive, imagistic. "They'll bang the door on his cock so hard it's going to turn black and drop off." (Logan)
Spec the **style**, not just frequency: which words, aimed at whom, sexual/scatological/blasphemous, and whether wit is required.

### 13. markers — fillers, tags, address (structured field)
The connective tissue; cheapest high-yield differentiator. Spec four palettes:
- **Openers**: Look / Well / So / Okay / I mean / Right / Listen / [none — starts mid-thought]
- **Tags**: right? / no? / yeah? / you know? / innit / isn't it / so it is / hm?
- **Concession style**: does this voice *ever* concede, and how? ("No, you're right" / "Fine." / "Sure, sure" / never)
- **Address**: how they name people — honorifics (Mr. Tully), endearments (sweetheart, baby), diminutives, insults-as-address (you invisible cunt), full names, or nothing.
Exemplars: Logan's dominance-listening "Uh-huh."; Hank's service-industry "Great great."; the Irish clause-final "so" — "I'll sit somewhere else, so." (Colm, *Banshees of Inisherin*); Mrs Jennings' "depend upon it" (*Sense and Sensibility*).

## Group D — SOUND (rhythm and interaction)

### 14. tempo & pause
`halting → deliberate → measured → quick → torrential`
- **halting**: hesitation inside the clause; self-repair. "Umm… no. Is that what you think?" (Sandra)
- **deliberate**: slow on purpose; pauses *before* speaking (weighing) or dead-center for weight.
- **quick**: fast volley, short gaps.
- **torrential**: speech outruns breath; listener can't get in. "Can you believe they had the nerve to trot Willie Pastrano into the ring before the fight?" → straight into the next thought (Cassius, *One Night in Miami*)
Also spec **pause placement**: before the line / mid-thought (searching) / trailing off… — each is a different psychology.

### 15. turn-taking & listening
`yields → volleys → holds-floor → interrupts → steamrolls`
- **yields**: waits, cedes on overlap, finishes last.
- **volleys**: trades cleanly, matches partner's length.
- **holds-floor**: monologue-prone; answers grow.
- **interrupts**: cuts in when they've heard enough (or too much).
- **steamrolls**: talks over; other people's turns are background noise.
Plus **listening behavior** — how they signal receipt (this is half the voice): minimal power-tokens ("Uh-huh." — Logan), silence-as-reply, echo-then-attack, enthusiastic agreement ("That sounds good." — Hank), challenge-by-question (Hunham grading a room: "No? Mr. Kountze? Mr. Smith?").

### 16. repetition
`none → self-keywords → echo-of-others → refrains → loops`
- **self-keywords**: reuses their own load-bearing words (reuse IS voice — don't synonym-cycle it away).
- **echo-of-others**: repeats your word back, usually to weaponize it.
- **refrains**: callback phrases across scenes.
- **loops**: repetition under emotion — "Bitch, really? Really? You gone up and have auditions after I told you explicitly not to? Really, bitch?" (Mercedes); "Great great." (Hank)

## Group E — ORIGIN (where the voice comes from) — structured, not sliders

Set only the layers that apply; each active layer gets the **intensity dial** (light / medium / heavy — feature budgets defined in `dialect-and-l1.md`).

- **Native variety / region**: unmarked · AAVE · Southern US · NYC · London (Cockney/MLE) · Irish · Scottish · other (describe). Features come from the kits in `dialect-and-l1.md` — grammar and idiom first, phonetic respelling essentially never (see the P-Valley exception below).
- **L1 if non-native**: French · Spanish · Russian · German · Mandarin · Arabic · other + **proficiency** (survival / functional / fluent-marked / near-native) + **code-switch triggers** (compatriots present, emotion spikes, counting/praying, missing word).
- **Education imprint**: unlettered · self-taught (book words slightly misapplied, hypercorrection, pride) · schooled · academic. Literacy errors are *systematic*, never random — "me don't want that" is a system (pronoun-case leveling), not noise; pick the system.
- **Era / generation**: slang vintage, references, formality floor. (Mrs Jennings' "depend upon it" does more period work than any costume.)
- **Class markers**: what money/status vocabulary they reach for, what they conspicuously avoid.

**The P-Valley exception, stated honestly:** the default rule (grammar/syntax/idiom, no eye-dialect) has a known exception class — writers rendering their own community with total phonetic commitment (Katori Hall's "I'on need nuthin' else", August Wilson). That is an auteur register earned by insider knowledge and consistency across every page. The skills default to grammar-first; full phonetic rendering only if the writer explicitly opts in, owns or has deeply researched the community, and commits everywhere (one respelled character in a standard-spelled cast reads as mockery).

**Developmental register** (Bella Baxter's "Bella not safe with you I think" → later baroque eloquence): treat as Origin with an arc — spec the stages and the trigger for each shift in Evolution Notes.

## Group F — ENGINE (open fields; the axes' why)

- **Signature move** (1 sentence, mandatory): the incongruity or mechanism that makes the voice. Hank: customer-service warmth wrapped around mob violence. Hunham: erudition as both weapon and hiding place. Logan: interrogative dominance — power spends words only to test people.
- **Obsessions / steering topics**: what they drag every conversation toward.
- **Metaphor source-domains**: where their images come from (scripture, sports, war, food, money, chess). A farmer and a surgeon can both say "cut your losses" — only one of them means the knife.
- **Taboos / avoidances**: what they will not discuss, and their deflection maneuver when it nears.
- **Catchphrases & tics — HARD CAP: 2.** More reads as sketch comedy. (House rule inherited from screenplay-dialogue: "use sparingly but consistently — one or two per character maximum.")
- **Never says**: words/registers alien to this mouth (therapy-speak in an 1870s homestead; "utilize" from a dockworker; an apology from Logan).
- **Want & self-image** (optional psychology hooks): what they want long-term, how they think they sound (delusion between the two is comedy or tragedy).

## Group G — MODULATION LAYER (states, not traits)

Baseline is who they are at rest; scenes bend the dial. In voice.md this is the **Under Pressure** section — mandatory, because a spec without it produces a monotone character.

**The general law: stress subtracts complexity and amplifies signature.** Vocabulary shrinks, sentences shorten and break, repetition rises; and the character's most-them feature gets *more* them (Hunham more ornate mid-insult, Hank more courteous mid-threat, Logan more interrogative mid-rage) — until the breaking point, where even the signature collapses into plain short true words ("The moment should have been less coherent and more emotionally clear" — John August).

Spec at minimum these states as deltas from baseline (which axes move, which direction, what leaks):
- **Anger** (hot or cold? louder or quieter? profanity rules change?)
- **Fear / stress** (L1 interference thickens one notch; hedges multiply or vanish?)
- **Grief / hurt**
- **Deceit** (their tells: over-precision? charm spike? tempo change?)
- **Tenderness / intimacy** (who unlocks it; what drops away)
- Optional: drunk, exhausted, with-their-mother, in-public — any state the story will visit.

Also inherit Mazin's mode frame (neutral / emotional / declarative / manipulative): a voice spec says *how this character sounds in each mode* — and never lets a character be emotional and declarative in the same breath.
