# Juno (2007) — Structural Beat Map
**Source:** /mnt/user-data/uploads/Screenplay planning/Ressources/Script Library/Feature Misc/RomCom/juno-script-pdf.pdf · **Writers:** Diablo Cody (title page; Dancing Elk Pictures production draft — Production White 1/12/07, this copy stamped "Production Revised Yellow 3/05/07") · **Format:** feature · **Genre:** indie comedy / rom-com hybrid (teen pregnancy dramedy) · **Length:** 101 pp printed (102 PDF pages; numerous OMITTED scene slugs)
**Framework lens(es):** PRIMARY — rom-com/indie hybrid beats + FOUR SEASONS chapter structure (the pregnancy as clock). SECONDARY — Save the Cat 15-beat quick pass.

Page citations use the script's printed page numbers. Percentages are of 101 printed pages.

## Logline
A caustic 16-year-old who gets pregnant by her best friend decides to give the baby to a picture-perfect suburban couple — and over the nine months of her pregnancy watches the "perfect" adult marriage collapse while discovering that the unremarkable boy who got her pregnant is the person who loves her exactly as she is.

## Structure overview
The script is structured on the **pregnancy itself as a nine-month clock rendered as four seasons**. The seasons are written directly into scene description (no chapter cards in this draft; the finished film adds them):

| Chapter | Pages | % of script | Season marker in text | Pregnancy stage | Act function |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FALL | 1–42 | 1–42% | "It's FALL." (p.1) | Conception (pre-film flashback) → confirmation → ~12 weeks (stated p.33–34; due May 4) | Act 1 + break into Act 2: discover, debate, decide (abortion → adoption), contract with the Lorings |
| WINTER | 43–68 | 43–67% | "It is now WINTER." (p.43); fresh snow p.64 | Second trimester; 5 months at p.44; kicks felt p.68 | Act 2A: deepening on both fronts — Juno/Mark intimacy grows (wrongly), Juno/Bleeker drifts, Vanessa validated at the mall |
| LATE WINTER → SPRING | 69–94 | 68–93% | "muddy, slushy snow" p.69; crocus p.88; prom season pp.72–76 | Third trimester; "almost eight" months p.71 | Act 2B + Act 3 part 1: collapse (Mark leaves Vanessa), dark night, epiphany, gesture, declaration — then birth |
| SUMMER (coda) | 100–101 | 99–100% | "a sparkling summer afternoon" (p.100) | Post-birth | Resolution: new equilibrium, the duet |

Key turns: **catalyst p.4 (4%)** (third positive test), **break into Act 2 p.21–24 (21–24%)** (adoption decision + finding the Lorings in the Penny Saver), **midpoint p.68 (67%)** (Vanessa feels the baby kick — the adoption plan's false victory — immediately after the "cold feet" doubt at p.57–58), **All Is Lost pp.80–82 (79–81%)** ("I'm leaving Vanessa"), **dark night p.86 (85%)**, **break into Act 3 pp.88–90 (87–89%)** (the Jiffy Lube note + Mac's speech + "I sort of already have"), **climax/union pp.92–98 (91–97%)** (declaration, birth, hospital-bed catharsis), **resolution pp.99–101**.

The rom-com beats proper (gesture → declaration → union) are compressed into pp.91–94 of the B-plot; the A-plot (what to do with the baby / the Lorings) supplies the crisis machinery.

## Save the Cat 15-beat quick pass
1. **Opening Image** (p.1): fall dusk, Juno alone before the discarded living-room set; "It started with a chair."
2. **Theme Stated** (p.4): Rollo — "one doodle that can't be undid" (irreversibility); love-theme restated by Mac at p.90 ("loves you for exactly what you are").
3. **Set-Up** (pp.1–15): Juno's voice, Leah, Bleeker, the MacGuff household, school ecosystem.
4. **Catalyst** (p.4): the third pink plus sign.
5. **Debate** (pp.5–20): abortion vs alternatives, climaxing in the clinic fingernails sequence.
6. **Break into Two** (pp.21–24): "I'm staying pregnant" → adoption → the Lorings chosen from the Penny Saver.
7. **B Story** (pp.8–10, 24–25, 60–63…): the Juno/Bleeker love story — the carrier of the theme; the Lorings are the A-plot vehicle.
8. **Fun and Games** (pp.25–57): pregnant teen in the adult world — telling parents, lawyer meeting, jam sessions, ultrasound, mall.
9. **Midpoint** (p.68): Vanessa feels the kick — false victory for the plan; stakes become personal (a mother, not a transaction). Paired with the p.57–58 "cold feet" shadow.
10. **Bad Guys Close In** (pp.69–79): internal bad guys — Juno/Mark intimacy escalates (phone, comic, slow dance), Bleeker drifts to Katrina, Bren's warning ignored.
11. **All Is Lost** (pp.80–85): "I'm leaving Vanessa"; the perfect-family fantasy dies; whiff of death = the marriage and (prospectively) the adoption.
12. **Dark Night of the Soul** (p.86 + p.89): sobbing on the highway shoulder; "I'm losing my faith in humanity."
13. **Break into Three** (pp.86–90): the Jiffy Lube note (A-plot fix) + Mac's speech → "I sort of already have" (B-plot fix) — both plots solved by the same insight: love as unconditional opt-in.
14. **Finale** (pp.91–99): gesture (Tic Tacs) → declaration (track fence) → birth → Vanessa a mother alone with the framed note → hospital-bed union.
15. **Final Image** (pp.100–101): summer duet — the loner of p.1 now half of a band.

## Storylines
- **A: The baby plot (Juno ↔ the Lorings).** Decision (fall), contract (p.31–42), validation (midpoint mall scene), betrayal (Mark), renegotiation by note ("If you're still in, I'm still in"), hand-off at the hospital. This plot owns the clock and the crisis.
- **B: The love story (Juno ↔ Bleeker).** Consummated before p.1, denied (p.7, p.63), wounded (p.9's "it wasn't your idea" lie), broken (locker fight p.73–76), repaired by gesture/declaration (pp.91–94), sealed by the held sob (p.98) and the duet (p.101). Carries the theme; receives the rom-com canon beats.
- **C: The mirror marriage (Mark ↔ Vanessa).** The adult "endgame couple" runs the anti-arc: prepared, wealthy, loveless → divorce (p.86–88). Splits into Mark's regression (loft, "cool guy") and Vanessa's earned solo motherhood (p.97–99).
- **D: The family (Mac, Bren, Leah).** Unconditional-love infrastructure: Bren's ultrasound defense (p.47–48) and boundary warning (p.59), Mac's thesis speech (p.90) and "on your terms" (p.98), Leah as co-conspirator in both the Penny Saver search and the Tic-Tac op.

## Structural devices
- **Four-seasons chapter clock:** season markers written into action lines (p.1 fall; p.43 winter; p.69 thaw; p.88 crocus; p.100 summer) map trimesters onto acts; the due date (May 4, stated p.34) is the third-act deadline that arrives on schedule.
- **The track team as season metronome:** the running Condors mark every time-jump (pp.9, 43, 69, 85, 100) and finally merge with plot when Bleeker's race intercuts the birth (pp.96–97).
- **Bookend V.O. + chair motif:** "It started with a chair" (p.1) / "It ended with a chair" (p.99) — conception chair → lawn recliner → empty antique rocking chair in the nursery.
- **Plant/payoff economy on tiny props:** orange Tic-Tacs (p.61 → p.91), the Jiffy Lube note (written p.86, delivered p.87–88 unread, framed p.99), hamburger phone (pp.5, 14, 24), "the song" from the basement (pp.24, 63, "theme" noodled p.86 → duet p.100–101), the nursery wall that "needs something" (p.65 → the framed note), Bren's Weimaraners (p.60 → p.100).
- **Fragmented flashback of the consummation:** the basement night is doled out in slivers (pp.1, 15, 62) so the backstory functions as suspense — what the sex meant is the mystery the locker fight (p.75) finally answers.
- **Duet grammar for intimacy:** who Juno makes music with tracks who she's wrongly/rightly bonded to — "Doll Parts" with Mark (p.41, interrupted by Vanessa) vs "Anyone Else But You" with Bleeker (p.100–101, uninterrupted).
- **The kick as love-barometer:** the baby won't move for Vanessa until she speaks to it (p.68) and kicks "super hard" whenever Juno looks at Bleeker (p.93) — one physical system measuring three relationships.
- **Voice-forward V.O.** that deflects sentiment, letting object payoffs and silent beats (the held sob p.98) deliver the sincerity.

## Deviations & tensions with the framework
- **The rom-com is the B-plot.** Canon beats (refusal, breakup, gesture, declaration, union) are all present but miniaturized and displaced onto Juno/Bleeker, while a decision plot (the baby) drives structure. The genre hybrid works because the same theme (unconditional love) resolves both.
- **No meet-cute; consummation precedes page one.** The lovers are already best friends who've already had sex — the story runs the rom-com backwards (sex → estrangement → courtship → declaration → chaste cheek-kiss).
- **The "wrong partner" is a married adult, and the wrongness is the point.** Mark occupies the rival/false-love slot, but the script frames the flirtation as boundary violation (Bren names it at p.59) rather than romantic option — a deliberate discomfort mainstream canon avoids.
- **The couple that breaks up at the climax is not the leads.** The adult mirror couple divorces; the union-equivalent set-piece (the birth) *severs* a bond (mother/child) instead of joining one — and that severance is the happy ending for two storylines at once.
- **The midpoint elevates the future antagonist's victim.** The mall scene re-values Vanessa at 67% so Mark's betrayal lands on her and the adoption can be saved by trusting *her* alone — an unusual bad-guys-close-in geometry where the "bad guy" is inside the dream couple.
- **Dark night and epiphany are nearly wordless and family-sourced:** the highway sob and a dad's kitchen speech replace the genre's public humiliation/confession apparatus.
- **The clock is biological, not calendrical-romantic** (contrast WHMS's New Year's rhymes): the deadline can't be moved, argued with, or missed, which is why the script never needs a manufactured ticking clock.

## Lessons for the knowledge base
1. **A hard biological/seasonal clock can replace plot machinery.** Stating the due date at 33% and marking trimesters as seasons gives every sequence a position on an unmovable timeline; weather, wardrobe (the waistband), and a jogging team communicate act breaks without a single title card of exposition.
2. **Rom-com canon beats still satisfy when displaced onto a B-plot and compressed.** Gesture → declaration → union occupy only pp.91–94 (≈4 pages) yet land at full weight because the A-plot spent 80 pages loading the theme they resolve; beat *placement* matters less than thematic charge.
3. **Starting after the consummation converts backstory into suspense.** Fragmenting the "meet" across three flashbacks makes the question "what did the sex mean?" the love story's engine, and lets the breakup fight (p.75) work as a revelation scene.
4. **Mirror couples are the hybrid's calibration tool.** The prepared-but-loveless adults vs the unready-but-devoted teens dramatize the thesis from both sides; giving the wronged half of the failed couple her own earned happy ending (Vanessa + baby + framed note, p.99) resolves the anti-romance without cynicism or schmaltz.
5. **In a voice-forward comedy, put the sincerity in objects and silences.** Tic-Tacs, a Jiffy Lube receipt, an empty rocking chair, a kick, a held sob — every emotional climax is carried by a planted prop or a wordless action, because the narrator's irony would deflate it if spoken; plant small, pay off at maximum load.
