# When Harry Met Sally... (1989) — Structural Beat Map
**Source:** /mnt/user-data/uploads/Screenplay planning/Ressources/Script Library/Feature Misc/RomCom/when-harry-met-sally-1989.pdf · **Writers:** Nora Ephron (title page; Castle Rock shooting draft with revised pages dated 8/88) · **Format:** feature · **Genre:** romantic comedy (two-hander) · **Length:** ~122 pp printed (numbering runs to p.122 with A-pages; pp.111–112 deleted; 137 PDF pages)
**Framework lens(es):** PRIMARY — rom-com beat canon (meet-cute, refusal/wrong partners, deepening, false union, breakup/dark night, grand gesture/declaration). SECONDARY — episodic years-spanning structure (time-jumps as act architecture) + documentary-interview interstitials as structural punctuation.

Page citations use the script's printed page numbers (visible on most pages). Percentages are of ~122 printed pages.

## Logline
Over twelve years and three chance meetings, two combative acquaintances who agree that "men and women can't be friends because the sex part always gets in the way" become best friends — until one night of comfort sex proves the thesis, destroys the friendship, and forces them to admit they were falling in love the whole time.

## Structure overview
The film is built as **four time blocks separated by explicit year-jumps**, each opened (or preceded) by a **documentary interview** with an old married couple. The three prologue episodes function together as an extended Act One; the "real" story (the friendship) doesn't begin until ~30%.

| Block | Pages | % of script | Time frame | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Doc interview #1 + Episode 1: the drive to New York | 1–16A | 1–13% | "TEN YEARS AGO" (1977, University of Chicago → NYC) | Anti-meet-cute; thesis stated; they part as non-friends ("Have a nice life") |
| Doc #2 + Episode 2: the airplane | 17–28 | 14–23% | "IT'S FIVE YEARS LATER" (1982, airport/plane) | Re-meet; both with wrong partners (Joe; Harry engaged to Helen); friendship offered, rule re-asserted, they part again |
| Doc #3 + Episode 3 → main story | 28–107 | 23–88% | "IT'S FIVE YEARS LATER" (1987 → winter 1988/89) | Both newly single; third meeting starts the friendship (Act 2 proper); deepening → false union → breakup |
| Separation → New Year's Eve climax + coda | 108–122 | 88–100% | Christmas → New Year's Eve (one year after NYE #1) | Dark night, grand gesture, declaration, kiss; leads become the final documentary couple |

Inside the 1987–89 block, the conventional act turns sit at: **friendship pact p.43–44 (~36%, true break into Act 2)**, **New Year's Eve almost-kiss p.63/64 (~52%, midpoint)**, **sex p.91 (~75%, false union)**, **wedding fight/slap p.104–107 (~86–88%, break into Act 3)**. The main story spans roughly 15 months anchored to two New Year's Eves — the calendar itself is the act architecture.

Documentary interstitials (structural punctuation): p.1 (married 50 years), pp.17–18 (high-school sweethearts reunited after 34 years), pp.28–29 (divorced, remarried 45 years later — "I knew"), pp.44–45 (five quick couples: what the other wore), pp.65–66 (fix-ups; the Jones Beach bickering couple), pp.72A–73 (same couple, Spanish Civil War rally), pp.100–101 (the broken-projector chain-of-chance story), and finally pp.121–122 — Harry and Sally themselves on the loveseat.

## Storylines
- **A: Harry ♥ Sally (the 12-year argument).** Three meetings (1977 drive, 1982 plane, 1987 bookstore), one friendship, one night of sex, one breakup, one declaration. The obstacle is never external — it is Harry's rule and both leads' self-protection.
- **B: Jess & Marie (mirror couple).** Introduced as the leads' confidants (p.34–36, p.29–33), fused in one scene by the backfired double fix-up (p.66–72), cohabiting through the wagon-wheel fight (p.76–79), married at p.103. They run the whole rom-com arc in the background at 3x speed and host the A-couple's crisis at their wedding ("If Marie or I had found either of them remotely attractive we would not be here today").
- **C: The documentary couples (thematic chorus).** Seven interstitial testimonies argue that real couples are made of time, chance and stubbornness. The A-story graduates into this storyline in the final scene.
- **D: The exes (offscreen wrong partners).** Joe and Helen do their damage largely by report — Helen's exit detonates Harry's Act 2 rage; Joe's engagement detonates the false union. Julian and Emily exist only to be the wrong people in the room.

## Structural devices
- **Card-announced time-jumps** ("TEN YEARS AGO", "IT'S FIVE YEARS LATER" x2) that compress the refusal phase into episodes; each jump resets the relationship at a higher level of readiness.
- **Documentary interstitials** as act punctuation, theme carriers, and final punchline (the leads become interviewees). They also license montage and time-compression inside the 1987–89 block.
- **Calendar rhyming:** two Christmases (shared tree p.60 → solo tree p.108) and two New Year's Eves (almost p.63/64 → declaration p.119–121); Auld Lang Syne literalized as "about old friends."
- **Split-screen telephony:** the twin-bed *Casablanca* call (p.47–49) and the four-way morning-after call (p.95–98) — intimacy and panic rendered in the same visual grammar.
- **Motif payoff economy:** "on the side"/ordering (p.9–10 → p.122), *Casablanca* (p.6–7 → p.47–49: Sally switches sides on the Bergman question), Washington Square arch (p.16 → p.117), the dip (p.63 → p.114 memory), high-maintenance speech (p.48) reprised as love-evidence in the list speech (p.120).
- **Scene-as-thesis-experiment:** nearly every set-piece (deli orgasm, batting cage, fix-up dinner) is a staged test of the "men and women can't be friends" proposition.

## Deviations & tensions with the framework
- **The meet-cute is triplicated and hostile.** Canon expects one charmed collision; here three meetings across 12 years each end in parting. The "cute" is retro-fitted by the final V.O. recap.
- **Act One is 36% long and mostly prologue.** The love story's real inciting incident (bookstore) arrives ~30%; the pre-1987 episodes would be dead weight if they weren't each restating and re-testing the thesis.
- **The refusal beat is a pact, not a rejection.** Instead of refusing each other, they jointly refuse romance — which converts every subsequent scene into dramatic irony.
- **No external antagonist or misunderstanding.** No lie, no rival at the climax, no airport chase against a wedding. The blocking force is a stated idea plus two personalities; the "villain" scene is a slap born of truth-telling.
- **False union causes the breakup** rather than a secret being revealed — the sex the thesis predicted is the thing that proves and then breaks the thesis.
- **B-couple marries before the leads,** inverting the usual sequence, and the wedding hosts the breakup — the genre's happiest set-piece is repurposed as the crisis venue.
- **Mockumentary frame inside a naturalistic rom-com** — a formal rupture the genre canon doesn't account for, resolved by folding the leads into the device.

## Lessons for the knowledge base
1. **A rom-com can replace its plot engine with a thesis.** State a testable rule early ("men and women can't be friends"), then structure every episode, set-piece, and time-jump as a new trial of that rule; the climax is the verdict, not just the kiss.
2. **Time-jumps work as act breaks when each era ends on the same unresolved note at higher stakes.** 1977 and 1982 both end with a parting; repetition-with-escalation lets 12 years read as three acts of one continuous argument rather than three stories.
3. **Interstitial testimony can carry theme so the leads can stay in comic denial.** The documentary couples say everything the protagonists can't; letting the leads join the device at the end makes the form itself the resolution.
4. **Anchor the midpoint and climax to the same calendar date.** NYE almost-kiss at ~52% and NYE declaration at ~98% create a promise/payoff rhyme, a built-in ticking clock, and free escalation (every holiday measures what's changed).
5. **In an internal-obstacle rom-com, the false union must trigger the dark night, and the declaration must re-price the mocked flaws.** The comfort-sex crisis (p.85–100) and the list speech (p.120) show the mechanism: every running gag is emotional escrow that the climax cashes as evidence of love.
